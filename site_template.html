ScrapeBench

Evaluating Legal Compliance of AI Agents in Website Scraping

Joseph Marvin Imperial1,2,3, Daniel Slate4, Noam Kolt5

1Pivotal Research, 2University of Bath, 3National University Philippines,4University of Notre Dame, 5Hebrew University of Jerusalem

Code & Data ↗Paper Link Forthcoming

ScrapeBench tests whether AI agents follow website scraping policies. We ask agents to scrape landing pages and examine how their actions change with the legal instructions and policy resources provided in the prompt.

We check two sources of website policy: the terms of service and robots.txt, a file containing instructions for automated crawlers.

Explicitly Prohibited (EP) websites explicitly prohibit scraping in their terms of service or robots.txt. Not Explicitly Prohibited (NEP) websites have no explicit prohibition in either source.

In this benchmark, Compliance means not scraping an EP website, while Over-Refusal means not scraping an NEP website.

Human Baseline

In a simulated task, we tested 180 human participants from Prolific where each made scraping decisions on 15 randomly sampled websites under P1, P4 or P7 conditions. A separate web app was used where humans are given the option to "scrape" or "not scrape" a website. Choosing “scrape” displayed a pre-saved website content and we de-briefed the participants that no actual scraping was performed during the activity.

Methods

Website Data

The benchmark uses 1,895 high-traffic, English-language websites accessible from the United States, drawn from the February 2026 Chrome Top Million Websites (CrUX) list.

The sample contains 1,626 EP websites and 269 NEP websites.

Legal Interpretation of Scraping Permissibility

EP and NEP describe the benchmark’s classification of website policies. The enforceability of terms of service and robots.txt varies across jurisdictions.

Therefore, we do not claim that any specific AI agent model's action, prompt, or outcome from this study constitutes a qualified unlawful act.

What Counts as Scraping +

A request for website content counts as a scraping attempt, even if the download fails. Requests for terms of service and robots.txt are tracked separately and do not count as requests for content.

Each trial evaluates a single agent attempting to scrape a website’s landing page. The study does not cover open-ended, multi-agent or multi-turn workflows.

Data and Reporting +

Compliance values reproduce the manuscript’s figures. Over-Refusal values use the corresponding aggregate results, rounded to one decimal place. Completed-trial denominators can vary. Confidence intervals are not displayed.

Fetched text was limited to 8,000 characters. Policy text supplied in the prompt could include up to 100,000 characters.
